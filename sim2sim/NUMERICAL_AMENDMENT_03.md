# Contact-mechanics audit and revised solver candidate

Independent drop-and-slide probe: 0.45 mm sphere, 1 mg mass, two free translations, fixed rotation, 0.2 mm initial clearance, horizontal speed 20 mm/s, gravity 9.81 m/s², friction 0.5/0.5, restitution zero. A rigid inelastic impact has enough Coulomb capacity to stop horizontal motion; its reference stopping position is about 127.71 µm.

Measured native TGS 16/4 at 62.5 µs: final x = 114.098 µm, minimum horizontal velocity = −11.301 mm/s, backward travel after the peak = 12.653 µm. Changing velocity iterations to zero did not remove this response. Reducing contact offsets to 2 µm per shape did not remove it; actual backend readback confirms those offsets were applied. Reducing only the friction threshold to 1 µm suppressed the impact friction impulse and produced 167.609 µm, so that is not adopted. The early conjecture that the stopping-distance difference alone demonstrated premature friction was disproved by the time trace: reversal occurs at impact, not during free fall.

Changing only solver type to **PGS 16/4**, with the original 20 µm offsets and 40 µm friction threshold, removes reversal: final x = 126.851 µm, minimum horizontal velocity zero, zero backward travel. This establishes a solver-dependent impact-friction discrepancy in this installed build and probe. It does not yet quantify the effect on the centipede or establish a universal PhysX defect.

MuJoCo's fixed 0.5 ms contact time constant converges to about 132.500 µm, with approximately 11 µm penetration in this probe. It is therefore not an exact rigid-contact counterpart. With numerical contact time constant **2 × internal step**, unchanged damping ratio 1 and impedance 0.9999, the result converges toward the rigid limit: 127.499 µm at 62.5 µs, 127.811 µm at 15.625 µs, and 127.656 µm at 7.8125 µs. The last result differs from the rigid reference by about 0.04%.

New candidate: PhysX PGS 16/4; MuJoCo implicitfast with contact time constant 2 × internal step. Keep all physical rigid-body properties, all physical PD gains, friction, restitution, initial state and scoring fixed. Keep the shared 62.5 µs command clock; independently refine internal integration. Start with the previously planned MuJoCo 15.625 / 7.8125 µs pair and native 62.5 / 31.25 µs pair. The original acceptance tolerances remain unchanged. The native refinement also refines analytic command samples as disclosed in amendment 02.

The complete original TGS CPU bank, original GPU calibration, all original MuJoCo coarse/fine results and completed soft-contact refinement cases are retained. In-progress superseded TGS long runs and fixed-contact-time-constant refinement workers may be stopped by verified owned PID, with explicit interruption receipts; uncompleted trajectories are not claimed as results. New PGS settings are selected from the independent mechanical probe, not by fitting gait displacement.

Primary documentation: [PhysX rigid-body solver semantics](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.0/docs/RigidBodyDynamics.html), [contact offsets](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.5.0/_api_build/classPxShape.html), [friction separation threshold](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/_api_build/classPxSceneDesc.html), [MuJoCo contact model](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/latest/computation/). New online margin/gap conventions differ across versions; current experiments use zero MuJoCo margin and gap and do not depend on that naming difference.
