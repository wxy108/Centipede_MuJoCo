# Common-contact branch scope — before its dynamic results

The preceding rigid-contact model remains unvalidated. It is not relabelled as a success. Its scalar friction, mass and PD alignment did not remove native contact-algorithm differences. The De Saxce correction removes the isolated sliding-normal anomaly, but its full-robot nonlinear solves fail at 1.47625 s / 1.367 s for dt125 / dt62.5. Those runs stopped and saved the unresolved state. Multiple root methods and tighter inner cone tolerances do not remove the saved dt125 frame residual; this does not prove mathematical nonexistence or a unique cause.

This separate branch uses one shared NumPy contact-force implementation and one exported collision-point definition in both engines. Native robot dynamics, joint limits, gravity, per-body mass/COM/inertia and physical PD remain native and unchanged. Native ground response is disabled to prevent duplicate forces. The new physical contact model is explicit normal compliance plus elastic tangential displacement with circular Coulomb return mapping. Its SI parameters and rationale are frozen in COMMON_CONTACT_V2.json before running it; they are an engineering baseline, not measured material properties or fitted gait parameters.

First compare one flat calibration gait at the matched dt31.25 µs / command250 µs. Run only short initialization checks before the full 6 s to catch frame, unit, contact-sign or stability errors. Then require all original physical readbacks, all eight unchanged holdouts, 10% cross-engine displacement, 5% half-step refinement, minimum 50 mm, completion and geometry-quality reporting before adoption. Additional force/moment consistency tests must validate the common wrench application. Do not claim this validates or equates the two engines' original contact solvers, historical optimizer results or RL training pipelines. Clean remains untouched.

## Semantic clarification added after the first results; no parameter or gate changes

The native asset's friction, restitution and contact-offset attributes remain recorded, but they do not generate forces when native ground response is disabled. In this branch, the shared spring/damper/friction function is the active contact definition. In particular, the native restitution attribute of zero must not be presented as the effective restitution of the compliant law; rebound emerges from stiffness, damping, effective inertia and integration. Rigid-geometry overlap represents equivalent contact deformation rather than a deforming mesh. Parameters are identical across engines but are not experimentally calibrated material properties.
