# Ground-contact erasure after frozen amplitude validation

Primary channel: independently draw each of the 19 bilateral leg pairs once at t=1.5 s with Bernoulli probability p. Selected pairs lose all ground collisions for the remainder of the trial. The event duration is the remainder of the declared trial; there is no restoration. This is persistent random pair loss, explicitly distinct from the old short burst channel. The user-facing x axis is p itself. Report actual fraction of pairs erased for every seed. Use the same 19 uniforms across wave numbers and across probability levels; never select masks using performance.

Preserve leg-body and leg-leg collisions. The old implementation disabled both collision masks entirely and therefore removed self-collisions as well as ground contacts. This new implementation assigns separate ground and robot collision bits, verifies that the p=0 pair-compatibility matrix exactly matches the original, and disables only compatibility between selected leg groups and the ground. Chassis-ground compatibility remains unchanged. No geometry, mass, PD, frequency, target, or friction coefficient is changed. p=0 must reproduce frozen nominal displacement and all original metrics exactly before erasure results are accepted.

No restoration occurs in the primary channel, avoiding a switch that reinstates a deeply penetrating foot. Do not pool it with burst erasure or describe it as time-varying transient dropout. A transient extension would require a separate declaration and an audited restoration rule. Contact-erasure simulations only start after amplitude qualification and selection are frozen.

Discovery uses all qualified waves, probabilities [0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1], and seeds 0..3. p=0 and p=1 are deterministic endpoints. Keep full-duration displacement, upright survival, engineering viability, body-ground support, and actual erased fraction separate. Retain failures; do not report conditional means over survivors. Incomplete numerical outcomes have no imputed full-duration score.

Choose candidate crossing pairs and brackets from discovery, then freeze that choice before testing new paired seeds 100..119. Confirmation requires low-minus-high displacement to have opposite signs with paired uncertainty intervals on the two sides, meaningful forward motion, and explicit stability/body-support interpretation. Recheck promising results with half timestep and longer duration using declared seeds. If evidence shows only dragging/collapse crossings or no crossing, state that result. The objective is not altered by noise.

Pair selection first prefers both waves remaining upright with mean chassis-contact fraction <=0.15 on both sides. The high wave must retain mean displacement >=max(5 mm, 10% of its baseline) and at least 75% upright discovery masks. If no pair has both waves clean, an independently confirmed crossing involving loss of low-wave stability remains a useful performance result but must be labeled as such. Do not suppress low-wave failures from means, and do not label a failure-mediated crossing as clean-locomotion evidence. Prefer the largest two-sided mean sign margin within the applicable category, before looking at confirmation masks.

Before any contact dynamics, refine the pair-ordering rule to match the PI's amplitude hypothesis: within each posture category, first prefer pairs whose already-frozen low-wave body amplitude is larger than the high-wave body amplitude, then maximize the two-sided discovery margin. Retain every explored pair and report whether this baseline amplitude ordering holds. This affects which pair receives held-out confirmation; it does not alter amplitudes, dynamics, scores, masks, or the all-wave discovery plots.
